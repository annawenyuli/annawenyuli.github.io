What Happens After Google Search? SEO, AEO, and the Fight to Be Chosen by AI

Search used to rank links. AI answers summarize the market. Agents may soon choose and transact. What happens to SEO, ads, attribution, and brand discovery when the interface changes?

The core shift: search results become answers, then actions

For roughly two decades, digital discovery followed a stable pattern: a consumer typed a query into a search engine, scanned a results page, clicked a website, and converted there. That architecture created an enormous ecosystem around SEO, SEM, Google Ads, analytics and search intelligence.

Three generations of discovery
Search eraHuman → Google → SERP → Website
→
Answer eraHuman → ChatGPT/Gemini → Answer → maybe Website
→
Agent eraHuman → Agent → research → choose → transact

The marketing problem changes at every step. In the search era, a brand competes to rank. In the answer era, it competes to be mentioned and cited. In the agent era, it competes to be selected.

SEO asks: “Can the search engine find and rank me?” AEO asks: “Will the answer engine trust and mention me?” Agentic discovery asks: “Will the machine choose me when it is authorized to act?”

How traditional search actually works

A search engine does not read the whole internet every time you type a query. It first crawls pages, builds an index, then uses ranking systems to decide which results to show.

GooglebotCrawler discovers and reads pages

Google indexStored representation of the web

Ranking systemsMatch query to relevant results
→
SERPSearch Engine Results Page

SERP simply means Search Engine Results Page: the page containing organic results, ads, AI Overviews, maps, shopping modules, videos and other surfaces. SEO tools historically became valuable because they measured this external system from the outside.

SEO, SEM and Google Ads are different businesses

SEO is the work of improving organic discoverability: technical crawlability, content, information architecture, links, authority and relevance. You do not directly pay Google each time an organic result is clicked.

SEM usually refers to paid search marketing. Google Ads auctions commercial attention. If a user searches “best travel credit card,” a bank can bid to show a sponsored result because the query expresses unusually strong purchase intent.

PlacementOrganicSponsored

Google Ads is itself becoming an AI system

The old operating model required marketers to choose keywords, write variants, manage bids and adjust campaigns manually. The direction of travel is toward objective-based automation: the marketer supplies a product, budget, goals and constraints; the system increasingly chooses query matching, bidding, audience, creative, landing page and channel.

This mirrors a wider pattern across advertising: operators move from manipulating every control to defining the objective function for an optimization system.

Why SEO created large software companies

Companies such as Semrush, Ahrefs, Conductor and BrightEdge do not know Google’s private ranking source code. Their value is observational. They crawl the web, monitor result pages, estimate search demand, track backlinks, compare competitors and surface opportunities.

Traditional SEO intelligence
GoogleOpaque ranking system
↔
Semrush / AhrefsObserve keywords, SERPs, links, competitors
→
Marketing teamDecides what to change

The business model is usually subscription software for teams, with enterprise versions adding governance, workflow, integrations and larger data access. Agencies monetize differently: retainers, projects or management fees for actually executing the optimization work.

AEO changes the unit from keyword to prompt

Answer Engine Optimization—also called GEO or AI Search Optimization—starts from the fact that conversational questions are much richer than traditional keywords.

Traditional keyword: best CRM Conversational prompt: We're a 200-person Series B SaaS company using HubSpot. We need stronger forecasting but do not want a year-long Salesforce implementation. What should we evaluate?

The keyword space is large but relatively structured. The prompt space is effectively unbounded: wording, context, geography, user history, industry and constraints can all change the answer. This is one reason AEO measurement is a harder data problem than rank tracking.

How Profound measures AI visibility

A common misconception is that an AEO platform must install a pixel on a brand’s website or receive private logs directly from model companies. That is not necessary for the core visibility metric.

1. Track a representative set of prompts

The platform maintains prompts relevant to a category or brand: “best corporate card,” “Ramp vs Brex,” “best expense management software for startups,” and so on.

2. Run those prompts across answer engines

Visibility measurement
Tracked promptsQuestions the brand cares about
→
Answer enginesChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, etc.
→
Observed answersMentions, order, citations, sentiment
→
Visibility metricsShare of responses / share of voice

If a tracked brand appears in 42 out of 100 relevant observed answers, a platform can calculate a visibility measure from those observations. That does not mean 42% of every global user saw the brand. It is a measurement over the monitored prompt set, platforms, regions and runs.

3. Separate “what people ask” from “what models answer”

A platform can generate or curate prompts itself, but that does not reveal real demand. Estimating prompt volume requires another dataset. One approach is opt-in consumer panel data: users explicitly participate in a research panel, behavior is aggregated and anonymized, and the resulting market-intelligence data can be licensed to measurement platforms.

Two different data questions
What are people asking?Consumer panel / demand data
+
What does AI answer?Repeated observations across answer engines

This separation is critical. Prompt-volume data estimates demand; answer observations estimate visibility.

Pixel, referral and UTM: downstream measurement

A pixel belongs to a different measurement layer. It is code or an event mechanism on the advertiser’s own property that reports what a user did after arriving: view content, add to cart, purchase, submit lead and so forth. Meta Pixel is a familiar example.

Referral traffic describes a user arriving from another website or platform. If someone clicks a link from an AI answer into a brand’s site, analytics may record that source as a referral.

UTM parameters are simply labels attached to a URL so analytics can identify the acquisition source, medium and campaign.

https://example.com/pricing ?utm_source=chatgpt &utm_medium=referral &utm_campaign=ai_discovery

Why AEO is harder than SEO

Prompt space explodes

A brand cannot simply track a few thousand stable keywords. It needs a taxonomy of intents, personas and conversational variations.

Answers are probabilistic

The same prompt can produce different brands, orderings and citations on different runs. Measurement therefore becomes statistical rather than a simple “rank #3.”

The brand’s own website is only one source

An answer engine may form its view from Reddit, G2, publishers, documentation, reviews, social media and the company’s own content. AEO therefore starts collapsing traditional boundaries between SEO, PR, content, community, reputation and product documentation.

Attribution is harder

An AI answer may influence a buyer who later types the brand name directly into a browser. That conversion can appear “direct” even though AI influenced the decision. The industry therefore needs better assisted-attribution models, not just last-click reporting.

Profound’s business model: Semrush logic for a new discovery layer

Profound did not invent the idea of selling observability to marketers. The strategic template already existed in SEO. Semrush and Ahrefs turned an opaque search ecosystem into a paid intelligence product: rank tracking, competitor monitoring, keyword discovery, backlink analysis and technical diagnostics. Profound applies a similar commercial logic to AI answers.

Old market vs. new market
SEO softwareKeyword → rank → backlink → opportunity
becomes
AEO softwarePrompt → mention → citation → visibility → opportunity

The key difference is that Profound is trying to move beyond measurement faster than the original SEO tools did. A pure visibility dashboard is vulnerable to commoditization because every major SEO platform can add AI tracking. The more defensible ambition is to own the workflow from observation to recommended action to execution.

Who buys it?

The public customer roster has included large consumer and enterprise brands such as Walmart, Comcast, Estée Lauder, ServiceNow, Zoom, Ramp, Cursor, MongoDB and Figma, alongside fintech and infrastructure companies. The buyer is generally not “the SEO person” alone. AEO can touch brand, communications, content, developer marketing, product documentation and corporate reputation because the model may learn about a company from the entire public web.

That cross-functional nature is one reason the category can become larger than classic rank tracking. A wrong AI answer about a software product is not merely a marketing problem; it can become a product-support, developer-experience or trust problem.

The competitive map: AI-native startups vs. SEO incumbents

Profound competes with two very different groups. AI-native companies such as Scrunch, Peec, AthenaHQ and others are optimized around generative answers from day one. Traditional platforms such as Semrush, Ahrefs, Conductor and BrightEdge start with years of search data, crawlers, enterprise customers and procurement relationships.

The incumbent threat is serious because the SEO workflow and the AEO workflow are adjacent. A marketing team would prefer not to buy separate systems for Google visibility, AI visibility, content opportunity, competitor monitoring and execution if one platform can integrate them.

Does Big Tech still need SEO—and will it need AEO?

Large technology companies absolutely still care about external discovery. A company such as Google is not only a search engine. It sells Google Cloud, Workspace, Pixel, Android, developer tools and many other products whose pages compete for search demand. Google has publicly discussed its own internal SEO work and has acknowledged that marketing organizations can work with external SEO companies.

The same logic applies to AI discovery. A famous brand may not need help establishing that it exists, but it does care about accuracy, positioning, product comparison, citations and recommendation context. If an enterprise buyer asks an AI which cloud is best for an agent workload, Google Cloud, AWS and Azure all care about how they are described. If a developer asks how MongoDB handles a technical configuration and the answer is wrong, the user may blame MongoDB rather than the answer engine.

For Big Tech, AEO therefore shifts from “make the model aware of my brand” toward “make sure the model represents my products accurately and competitively across an increasingly important discovery channel.”

Why Google cannot fully replace a neutral AEO layer

Google can build excellent analytics for performance inside Google AI Mode. OpenAI can expose publisher or brand analytics inside ChatGPT. But a global brand wants one comparative view across many surfaces.

This is analogous to financial markets: an exchange knows its own market better than a third party, but cross-market information layers still have value because customers need a consolidated view.

Why the SEO incumbents cannot simply “flip a switch”

The transition is harder than adding a column called “AI visibility.” Traditional SEO systems were built around relatively stable keywords, ranked URLs and click-through traffic. AEO needs statistical sampling across prompts, models, regions and repeated runs; it needs to reason about mentions rather than clicks; and it often has to observe content sources that influence an answer even when the brand’s own site never receives a visit.

It also expands the organizational buyer. SEO can live primarily within growth or search teams. AEO often touches brand, PR, content, developer relations and product because external sources influence model beliefs. Incumbents have the data advantage, while AI-native startups have the architecture and positioning advantage.

Profound’s competition is larger than other AEO startups

There are two strategic groups. The first is AI-native AEO software. The second—and arguably more important—is the incumbent SEO stack expanding into AI discovery.

GroupExamplesStructural advantage
AI-nativeProfound, Scrunch, Peec, AthenaHQ and othersDesigned around AI answers from day one
SEO incumbentsSemrush, Ahrefs, Conductor, BrightEdgeExisting customers, crawlers, search data, workflows
Platform ownersGoogle, model / answer-engine companiesFirst-party visibility inside their own surface

The strategic reason a neutral third party can still exist is cross-platform measurement. Google can explain performance inside Google. A model company can explain its own surface. A brand still needs one place to compare ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude and whatever comes next.

AEO is probably not the end state

The deeper transition begins when the AI no longer gives five choices but is authorized to act.

What brands optimize for over time
SEORank me
→
AEOMention and cite me
→
Agent optimizationChoose and transact with me

At that point, marketing infrastructure starts merging with product infrastructure. An agent will care about structured product data, inventory, price, reviews, delivery, trust, return policy, APIs, authentication and transaction reliability. The machine buyer must be able to understand and act on the offering.

The future marketing question therefore becomes less “how do I make a page persuasive?” and more “how do I make my business legible, credible and executable to both humans and machines?”
